Neocaridina Shrimp


General Information

  • Neocaridina shrimp are facinating creatures that come in many colors, shapes, sizes, and personalities
  • Neocaridina Shrimp are relatively peaceful, calm, and great additions to an aqaurium
  • Neocaridina Shrimp tend to be very hardy, tolerating a wide range of PH and temperature
  • They are often found grazing along the aquarium's bottom, alaways searching for food
  • There are many types of neocaridina shrimp, so they will be arranged into different categories

Cherry Shrimp

Cherry shrimp are my personal favorites since they are easy to breed, raise, and come in such a beautiful color. As the name implies, cherry shrimp are cherry like in color. Baby cherry shrimp can grow up in fresh water, and reach full size in 3-5 months.
Once fully grown, Cherry shrimp are about 1 to 1.5 inches in length. Females are usually larger and more vividly colored, reaching the upper end of the length.
They can live in temperatures between 60-82°F, but I've notived they prefer warmer waters.

Amano Shrimp

Amano shrimp live in freshwater tanks and enjoy grazing on algae and sifting through substrate for food. Although mature amano shrimpcan endure freshwater conditions, if you look to raise baby amano shrimp they must be in brakish water.
Baby amano shrimp are born as larva that are very small, they cannot live in freshwater untill mature. Raising Amano shrimp tends to be a challenge since they are suceptible to bacteria in the water.
Amano shrimp grow to a size of 1.5 to 2.5 inches, making them significantly larger than cherry shrimp. Females are generally larger and broader, often reaching 2-3 inches.
The ideal, most comfortable range is generally considered to be 70°F to 78°F.
